Family 1 • Bullish Playbook

When You Think Google Goes UP by 20% ($100 → $120)

0. Buy the Stock ("Buy the Lego Boxes")

A limited-edition Lego set costs $100 today. You spend $10,000 to buy 100 boxes. When the price hits $120 (+20%), you sell them for $12,000 and make +$2,000 (+20%). Simple, no expiration clock.

1. Buy Call Options ("Buy Price-Lock Coupons")

Instead of buying boxes, you buy $5 coupons that let you buy 1 box for $100 anytime in the next 60 days. With $10,000, you buy 2,000 coupons (20 contracts). At $120, each coupon is worth $20 - turning $10,000 into $40,000 (+$30,000 profit / +300%)! Catch: If the box stays at $100, the coupons expire worthless (-$10,000).

2. Bull Call Spread ("Cheaper Coupon with a Cap")

You buy the $5 coupon (to buy at $100), and help pay for it by selling another kid a $1 coupon for any gains above $110. Now each coupon costs you $4, so $10,000 buys 2,500 coupons. You make more money than regular Calls on a +10% move (+$15,000 profit at $110), but your payout stops growing above $110.

3. Sell a Cash-Secured Put ("Be the Insurance Company")

A nervous owner pays you $500 cash today if you promise: "If the price drops below $100, I'll buy your 100 boxes for $100 each." You lock $10,000 in a safe as collateral. When Google rises to $120 (or stays flat at $100!), nobody forces you to buy, and you keep the +$500 (+5%).

Family 2 • Bearish Playbook

When You Think Google Goes DOWN by 10% ($100 → $90)

0. Short the Stock ("Borrow & Sell the Box")

You borrow 100 Lego boxes from a friend, sell them today for $100 each ($10,000), wait for the price to drop to $90, buy 100 boxes back for $9,000, return them, and pocket +$1,000 (+10%). Danger: If the box rockets to $250 instead, you still have to buy 100 boxes back at $250 - your potential loss is unlimited.

1. Buy Put Options ("Buy Price-Floor Coupons")

You pay $5 per coupon for the right to force someone to buy Google from you at $100. With $10,000, you buy 2,000 coupons. At $90 (-10%), each coupon is worth $10 - turning $10,000 into $20,000 (+$10,000 profit / +100%). At $80 (-20%), it turns into $40,000 (+300%)! And your worst-case loss is strictly capped at your $10,000 bet.

2. Bear Put Spread ("The -10% Target Winner ★")

Because you specifically expect a -10% drop to $90 (not a -30% crash), you buy the $100 Put coupon ($5) and sell the $90 Put coupon ($1) to cut your cost to $4 per coupon (2,500 coupons total). At $90 (-10%), your $10,000 turns into $25,000 (+$15,000 profit / +150%) - beating plain Put Options by $5,000!

3. Sell a Call ("Be the House Against a Rally")

An optimistic trader pays you $500 cash today for a $100 Call coupon. If Google drops 10% to $90 - or even if Google stays completely flat at $100 - their coupon expires worthless and you keep the +$500 (+5%).

Family 3 • Stock Owner Playbook

When You Already Own 100 Shares ($10,000): Rent & Crash Insurance

1. Covered Call ("Rent Out Your Shares")

You own 100 boxes of Lego at $100. You sell someone a $110 Call coupon for $300 cash. If Google stays below $110 this month, you keep your 100 shares AND pocket the +$300 rent. If Google rises past $110, you sell your shares at $110 (+$1,000 gain) plus keep the $300 rent (+$1,300 total / +13%).

2. Protective Put ("Crash Insurance")

You own 100 shares at $100 and are scared of a crash, so you pay $300 for a $95 Put coupon. If Google crashes to $70 (-30%), your Put guarantees you can sell your shares for $95 - capping your worst-case loss at -$800 (-8%) while keeping unlimited upside if Google rallies!

3. The Collar ("Free Crash Insurance")

Combine both! You collect +$300 rent by selling a $110 Call, and immediately spend that $300 to buy a $95 Put. Net option cost: $0. Your 100 shares now have a guaranteed floor at $95 (-$500 max loss) and a cap at $110 (+$1,000 max gain).

Family 4 • Volatility Playbook

When You Don't Know the Direction: Straddles & Iron Condors

1. Long Straddle ("Bet on an Explosion")

A huge court ruling is tomorrow. Google will either rocket +20% or crash -20%, you just don't know which. You buy both a $100 Call ($5) and a $100 Put ($5) for $10 total (1,000 pairs with $10,000). If Google moves more than 10% in either direction, you win big! At -20% ($80) or +20% ($120), your $10,000 turns into $20,000 (+$10,000 profit).

2. Long Strangle ("Cheaper Explosion Bet")

Same idea as a Straddle, but you buy cheaper out-of-the-money coupons: a $105 Call ($2.50) + $95 Put ($2.50) for $5 total (2,000 pairs with $10,000). Because you control twice as many shares, a ±20% move ($80 or $120) turns $10,000 into $30,000 (+$20,000 profit / +200%)!

3. Iron Condor ("Bet It Goes Nowhere")

You think Google will stay boring between $90 and $110 all month. You sell a Put Spread below $90 and a Call Spread above $110, collecting +$4,000 upfront on $10,000 of collateral. If Google finishes anywhere inside the $90-$110 goalposts, all coupons expire worthless and you keep the +$4,000 (+40%)!

3 Golden Rules Before Placing Your First Options Trade

1. The 90/10 Sizing Trick

Never put 100% of your $10,000 into Call or Put coupons. If you keep $9,000 in safe cash and put just $1,000 into Calls, a +20% move in Google still makes you +$3,000 (more than owning $10,000 of stock!), while your absolute worst-case loss is strictly capped at $1,000.

2. Match the Strategy to the Move Size

Expecting a moderate move (5% to 12%)? Use a Spread (Bull Call Spread or Bear Put Spread) so your coupon is cheaper and your breakeven is closer. Expecting a massive 20%+ breakout or crash? Use plain Calls, Puts, or a Strangle so your upside isn't capped.

3. Buy Enough Time (60+ Days)

Options melt like ice cubes as expiration approaches (called Theta / Time Decay), especially in the final 30 days. Buying 60 to 180 days of time gives your thesis room to play out, and remember: you can sell your option for a profit on any day before expiration.
